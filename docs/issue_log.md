## Assumptions
- When a user has several open subscriptions to the same business, the most recent active one is the current subscription; older open ones were superseded and never closed (issue #20).
- Subscriptions with placeholder prices would have paid the standard grid price for their plan and billing cycle (issue #18).
- lifetime_deal_fake is a monthly price locked for life (recurring revenue), not a one-time payment (issue #17).
- Session durations of exactly -15, 86,400 (1 day) and 604,800 (7 days) seconds are placeholders, not real sessions (issue #25).
- Runtime values -1 and 99999 are placeholders (common conventions for unknown or missing), not real lengths (issue #7).
- Content in BIZ_1 and BIZ_2 is not creator-produced (issue #1).

## Methodology notes
- Row outcomes in the clean layer: **keep** (valid), **keep + flag** (plausible but questionable; stays in the clean table with a short reason in a single `dq_flag` column), or **quarantine** (invalid; moved to a `quarantine_` table with a reason). Reconciliation: raw rows = clean rows + quarantined rows. Metric definitions state whether flagged rows are included.
- Exact distinct counts are used throughout; approximate counts produced impossible results (more distinct IDs than rows). Example: the approximate profile estimated 298 distinct content IDs, suggesting duplicates; the exact count confirmed 300 unique IDs (no duplicates). Similarly, users: approximate 778 unique IDs vs. exact 800; creators: approximate 39 vs. exact 40; engagement events: approximate 5,600 vs. exact 6,000 (which would have suggested 400 duplicate events that do not exist).
- Pattern lists only find expected placeholders: the missing-value check (which looked for "N/A", "unknown", etc.) missed age_band = '999' and primary_device = 'unknown_device'; reading the full list of values in each column caught them. Every category column is reviewed value by value.
- Before reading engagement as evidence about a group (e.g., test-status or stale-trial subscriptions), it is compared against a baseline of all subscribers by status. This showed engagement does not separate paying from non-paying customers here.
- "Missing" is checked three ways: nulls, blank text, and placeholder text ("N/A", "null", "unknown", "-").

## Observations (not data-quality issues)
- Monthly active users (raw, before cleaning) grew steadily: from 1–5 per month in 2021 to 431 in August 2026 (+13% vs. July). Events per active user also rose (about 1.6 in August 2026 vs. about 1.0 in 2021–2022). Every month has activity from January 2022 onward; 2021 has gaps (low volume).
- 92% of user–business pairs (718 of 778) have a single subscription, so subscription history is limited. Where history exists, 11 pairs go "canceled → active": customers who canceled and came back (win-backs).
- Engagement is similar across all subscription statuses: 86–88% of paid subscribers were active in the last 90 days, whether active, paused, canceled, trialing or test status. Engagement cannot be used to infer whether someone is a paying subscriber in this data.
- Paused subscriptions are mostly long-running (85 of 96 paused for over 30 days; median 390 days). Not an error, but whether paused counts as an active subscriber is a metric-definition decision.
- Monthly-billed subscriptions are canceled far more often (50.2%) than annual (22.8%), Free (16.3%) or lifetime (12.8%). A retention signal worth exploring, though groups differ in age, so it is a hint, not proof.
- About 1 in 3 paid subscriptions shows no activity on the business it pays for (similar across statuses), so "active subscriber" and "engaged subscriber" are very different numbers.
- 111 users (13.9%) are in the 13-17 age band, the third-largest group. Not a data error, but minors often have special rules (consent, payments, content), so their subscriptions and engagement may need separate handling or a caveat.
- BIZ_3 creator signups are flat (9, 8, 8, 9 new creators per year in 2022–2025; 3 in 2026 through August), while BIZ_3 content grew sharply (27 items in 2025, 41 in 2026 through mid-September). Content growth appears to come from existing creators publishing more, not from more creators. The lower 2026 signup count is too small and partial to read as a slowdown.
- Anomalies cluster in the lowest IDs: the 4 creators with placeholder follower counts are CR_0001 to CR_0004, and the test content includes CNT_00001 and CNT_00002. Early records may be seed or test records from system setup. Useful as a lead for the remaining audits, not as a rule for excluding records.
- Subscriptions start well after each business launched, while events start soon after: BIZ_1 launched 2021-01-01, first event 2021-01-09, first subscription 2021-07-24; BIZ_2 launched 2021-06-15, first event 2021-08-10, first subscription 2021-12-01. This could be a free period after launch or subscription history that starts later than engagement history. Not relevant to the August 2026 reporting month, but subscription trends should not be read as starting at launch.
- Engagement is spread very evenly across content (9–31 events per item, median 18–20 in every business). Real content engagement is usually highly skewed (a few hits, a long tail), so this dataset can't support "top content" or popularity insights; any such analysis would mostly reflect noise.
- Content volume is low: typically 0–5 items per business per month. Monthly content counts by business are too noisy to report as a KPI; use quarterly or yearly views.
- BIZ_3 (creator_content) is the fastest-growing content source: 11 items in 2022, 10 in 2023, 16 in 2024, 27 in 2025, and 41 in 2026 through mid-September.
